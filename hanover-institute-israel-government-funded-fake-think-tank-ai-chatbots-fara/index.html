P4The Hanover Institute: a think tank paid for by Israel's government, written for the chatbots

Domain registered 26 Jun 2026 · About page read 27 Oct 2026
  • Its About page: paid for via “the Israel Government Advertising Agency,” registered as foreign-agent material — so it “does not call itself independent, nonpartisan or neutral.”
  • 124 reports, 560,000 words, nine days. Headlines written as the questions people ask ChatGPT.
  • It says it was “established and publishing before that funding existed.” The contract is dated 30 April 2026; the website's domain was registered on 26 June.

The Hanover Institute for Public Policy says it “studies the inputs fueling antisemitism in the United States,” and its own About page discloses that its materials are distributed by Piro, Inc. on behalf of Havas Media Germany “acting for the Israel Government Advertising Agency (LaPam),” under Foreign Agents Registration Act registration 7732 — which is why, it says, it “does not call itself independent, nonpartisan or neutral.” Filings with the US Justice Department show a $900,000 “Digital Storytelling Pilot” dated 30 April 2026 and a $100,000 “Fact-Based Content Initiative” on behalf of Israel's Foreign Ministry; the Guardian counted 124 reports of more than 560,000 words in nine days in August 2026, nearly all headlined as questions a user might type into a chatbot, from a site with no named staff, no bylines and, it found, no apparent legal entity.

Credit where due: the only outfit on this desk that admits who pays for it. In the footer.
⚠ Hanover's disclosure is real and legally required: Piro, Inc. is registered under FARA (no. 7732, received 2 June 2026) for Havas Media Germany on behalf of LaPam. It says the funder “does not select topics, does not see drafts” and that some of its reports run against the funder's interests — and some do record facts unfavourable to Israel. Two things don't fit its account: the work order predates the website, and an “antisemitism” institute files reports on Gaza's siege, East Jerusalem and the right of return under “Israel & Anti-Zionism.” Claims that its reports are AI-written rest on detection tools (Pangram, GPTZero) cited by 404 Media and Responsible Statecraft, which are not proof. It has no Wikipedia article as of 27 Oct 2026.

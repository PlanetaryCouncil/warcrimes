P7The Henry Jackson Society's human-shields report: four in ten of its links go to the Israeli army

Report published April 2025 · read and counted 19 Nov 2026 · Last updated 23 Nov 2026
  • 132 of 325 links go to the Israeli army — its Telegram, X, video server and website. One link goes to Gaza's authorities.
  • Its authors: a former British Army major who visited Gaza twice in 2024, and an HonestReporting board member.
  • Its money: 47% of the UK charity's 2024 income was a single donation from its US arm, Henry Jackson Society Inc, whose donors aren't published.

“Hamas's Human Shield Strategy in Gaza” (April 2025, 87 pages) by Andrew Fox and Salo Aizenberg for the Henry Jackson Society, a London think tank, calls itself the “missing chapter” in UN and NGO reporting on Gaza. Of the 325 links in its text, 132 (41%) go to the Israeli army's own channels and 7 more to Israeli state accounts; one goes to Gaza's authorities. One author sits on the board of HonestReporting, the other went into Gaza in 2024 as, by AOAV's account, a guest of the Israeli army. Almost half the charity's 2024 income came from its US arm, whose donors it doesn't name. Hamas fighting from among civilians is documented; this report is a prosecution brief built largely from one party's evidence.

The question it asks is fair: Hamas does fight from tunnels under homes, and most UN and NGO reports say little about how. The method isn't. A brief whose main witness is the army being accused, and whose findings say the army's videos “prove without doubt”, is advocacy, not the missing chapter. Hasbara with footnotes.

What it says

The report argues that Hamas used “ten distinct human shield strategies”, that its leaders have admitted it, and that the UN and NGOs “deliberately disregard” it, so that “all of the evidence documented in this report is absent from UN and NGO reports.” It calls itself the “missing chapter” in those reports. One of its key findings:

“The IDF has provided thousands of high-quality videos and photographs of its activities in Gaza that prove without doubt that Hamas has militarised large portions of the civilian infrastructure of the Gaza Strip.”

That sentence is the method in miniature: the accused party's footage, accepted as proof.

Whose evidence

Counted from the report's PDF text: 325 links, 313 of them different. Some are Hamas's own material relayed by others, and some Israeli army posts are footage of real tunnels and real weapons. Who wrote it

  • Andrew Fox, research fellow at HJS, 16 years in the British Army, left the Parachute Regiment as a major. The report says he “visited Gaza twice” in 2024; foreign journalists weren't allowed in, and AOAV describes him as “the guest of the Israel Defence Forces (IDF) on trips to Gaza.” reported
  • Salo Aizenberg has written reports for NGO Monitor and, in the report's own words, “serves on the board of HonestReporting” — see our page on HonestReporting. verified
  • Peer reviewers: two named in the acknowledgments. No review by anyone outside the authors' circle is mentioned.
  • Fox's previous HJS report, “Questionable Counting” (December 2024), attacked Gaza's casualty figures. Researchers Gabriel Epstein and Michael Spagat, writing for AOAV, found its key findings “contain some truth” but that the evidence “does not support the dramatic charges” it makes (AOAV). reported

Who pays

  • The UK charity (no. 1140489): income £1,255,010 in 2024, £1,239,172 of it donations (Charity Commission). Of that, £579,403 was one donation from Henry Jackson Society Inc, its US arm — 47% of the year's income. The accounts say the charity “relies on continued support from Henry Jackson Society Inc, a US entity” (2024 accounts). verified
  • The US arm, Henry Jackson Society Inc, New York: about $561,000 in contributions in 2023 (ProPublica, from IRS filings). US charities need not publish their donors, and it doesn't. verified
  • Some donors show up in the givers' own filings: the American Endowment Foundation, the Goodman Family Supporting Foundation and DonorsTrust (InfluenceWatch); the Marc E. Leland Foundation, the Newton & Rochelle Becker Charitable Trust and Vanguard Charitable (Grantable). Several are donor-advised funds, which pass on money without naming who gave it. reported
  • Earlier: a 2015 Spinwatch study traced HJS donors to Conservative and pro-Israel funding networks (University of Bath record); in 2020 openDemocracy reported the Home Office paid HJS £83,452 for a report on Islamist terrorism (openDemocracy). reported

We found nothing showing who paid for this report, or that the Israeli government did. HJS says its donors are entitled to privacy. That is legal, and it is also why we can't tell you more.

What it gets right, what it gets wrong

  • Right: Hamas built tunnels under homes, schools and hospitals, fired from populated areas, and its leaders have said things that are on the record. The UN's genocide-related reports spend little space on how Hamas fights. Those are fair points.
  • Overstated: “Never asserted” and “all of the evidence… is absent” are sweeping. UN reports did record allegations of armed groups operating among civilians, as the report itself concedes four times; it dismisses them for using the word “allegation”, which is what an unverified claim is.
  • Careless: its key UN search is dated “from 7 October 2023 through 1/15/2023” — a range that ends before it starts. Probably a typo for 2025; the count it supports is the report's headline number.
  • Wrong on the law: shielding is a war crime (ICRC rule 97), but it doesn't hand the attacker a blank cheque. Civilians used as shields are still civilians; precautions and proportionality still apply to every strike. The report's case that Hamas shields says nothing about whether any given Israeli attack was lawful — see Nasser, November 2026.

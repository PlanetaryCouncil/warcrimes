Official government sources: exercise extra caution

Read 29 Oct 2026 · every claim below links to its source
  • Israel is a party to this war and is accused of war crimes in it. Its statements are one side's case, not neutral evidence.
  • Its public-diplomacy (hasbara) budget roughly quintupled, to about $730 million, in 2026.
  • On several incidents on this site, the first official account was later contradicted by video, forensics or the army's own inquiry.

Every incident page here quotes the Israeli government's own response, linked to the original wherever we can find it. We do that because a record that leaves out one side's case isn't a record. We also file those statements in their own box, apart from the independent sources, with one instruction: exercise extra caution. That isn't a claim that every official statement is false; many are accurate. It is the standard any court applies to an interested party: hear it, then check it. The same standard applies to the Hamas-run authorities in Gaza (example).

Trust is earned by evidence, not by letterhead.

The budget

In March 2026 the Knesset approved roughly $730 million for public diplomacy in the 2026 budget: nearly five times the $150 million of the year before, which was itself about 20 times pre-war spending. Foreign Minister Gideon Sa'ar: “It should be like investing in jets, bombs and missile interceptors.” reported — Times of Israel / JTA, 29–30 Apr 2026

The Quincy Institute counts more than $1 billion in direct public-diplomacy spending since 7 October 2023, and more than $100 million in FARA-registered spending in the US since 2023. reported — “The Eighth Front”, 24 Jul 2026

Some of that money buys voices that don't say who pays them. The Hanover Institute looks like an independent think tank; its US filings show work orders “on behalf of the Israel Ministry of Foreign Affairs.” verified — FARA Exhibit A/B

What “extra caution” means in practice

  1. Ask for the original. A file, a video, a log, not a crop or a summary.
  2. Look for independent corroboration. One government letter repeated by twenty outlets is still one source.
  3. Check whether the first account has changed. The first statement is often the one most widely shared, and the least checked.
  4. Read official words as a claim, not a finding. Attribute them: “the IDF said”, never just “it happened”.
  5. Apply the same rule to every party. Hamas-run ministries, too, are a party to the war.

H4The 2022 Sinwar memo: what is reported, what we don't know

Dated 24 Aug 2022 · recovered May 2025 · published 11–12 Oct 2025
  • No original files released for basic digital forensics. No uncropped page, no file, no EXIF, no hash, no device — only crops (one edited in Photoshop) and a phone photo of a paper page.
  • Selective framing. The independent translation puts the knife line on soldiers; Israel's institute and Foreign Ministry made it “civilians.”
  • Timed release. The report was ready by 3 August 2025, held for ten weeks, and published days after the ceasefire deal.

Israeli officials say a six-page handwritten Arabic memo dated 24 August 2022, attributed to Hamas Gaza leader Yahya Sinwar, sets out the plan for the 7 October 2023 attack: bulldozed fence breaches, waves of attackers, other factions told only after it began, and deliberately filmed violence including the burning of “an entire neighborhood, kibbutz, or something similar.” According to the New York Times (11 October 2025), an Israeli military unit found it in May 2025 as an image on an air-gapped computer used by his brother Muhammad Sinwar; every step from the bunker to publication — the find, the copy, the handwriting samples, the intercepts said to corroborate it — passed through the Israeli state, and no independent forensic examination of the file or the device has been made public.

They kept the laptop and sent out the crops — and hoped nobody would ask for the file. We're asking.
⚠ This page does not question that the 7 October attack happened or that atrocities were committed in it; those are documented independently (see H1). The question here is narrower: is this document what Israel says it is, and what does it prove? Israel is a party to the war and is accused of war crimes in it, so its evidence is weighed like any party's: don't trust, verify. What we noticed reading it

  • Soldiers become “civilians.” In the NYT translation the knife line is about soldiers: “opening fire on soldiers at point-blank range, slaughtering some of them with knives.” The Israeli institute's version says “slaughtering people with knives”; its summary says the memo instructs operatives to film themselves “indiscriminately slaughtering civilians”; the Foreign Ministry's post says “the slaughter of civilians.” The burning of neighborhoods and kibbutzim is explicitly aimed at civilian communities; the knife line, in the independent translation, is not. verified
  • The most-quoted line isn't from the memo. “Slaughter them. End the children of Israel” is, per the NYT, from an intercepted radio call on 7 October — a Gaza City commander called Abu al-Baraa, near Kibbutz Sa'ad — not from the memo. It circulates as a memo quote. verified
  • Draft notes left in. Footnote 9 of the institute's published report reads, in full: “This is pretty much footnote 6.” Footnote 6 cites a reading of Sinwar's 2004 novel, not the memo. verified
  • An open archive? Footnote 1: “The documents mentioned in this study can be viewed at the archives” of the institute. We found no report of an independent examiner doing so. open

Two things follow. First, the institute had the memo cropped and its report prepared by 3 August 2025 — about ten weeks before it published on 12 October, days after the ceasefire deal. Second, the Foreign Ministry's photo shows a handwritten page on paper, while the NYT says the computer “held an image.” Either it is a printout of that image, or there is a paper original that nobody has accounted for.

What we don't know

  1. Are the page images scans or photographs — and who made them, when?
  2. Was the computer encrypted or password-protected, and how was it opened?
  3. Has any file metadata been disclosed? Has a forensic disk image with a hash been offered to anyone outside Israel?
  4. Does a paper original exist? The Foreign Ministry's photo shows page 1 on paper, in a sleeve. Is that a printout, or the original?
  5. What is the chain of custody from the bunker to the newsroom?
  6. The institute's appendix shows five images; the memo is described as six pages. Is anything missing?
  7. Which translation is right where they differ — soldiers, or “people”? The Arabic images are public; an independent Arabic reader could settle it.
  8. Where are the raw intercept recordings, and has anyone outside Israel authenticated them?
  9. Were other files on the same computer withheld?
  10. Why five months between recovery and publication — and why hold a report that was ready by 3 August until days after the ceasefire deal?

None of these is hard to answer for whoever holds the laptop.

How to weigh it

For authenticity: the handwriting comparison; Hamas sources recognising the style; instructions that match 7 October (bulldozed fence breaches, attack waves, factions joining later, homes burned, atrocities filmed and broadcast); handwriting fits Sinwar's known avoidance of electronics. For caution: one custodian (the Israeli military); Israeli-only attribution; a match made from images; no file, device or metadata; an Israeli translation that is harsher than the independent one; the timing; and the record — in September 2024 supposed Hamas documents about Sinwar, published by the Jewish Chronicle and Bild, were found to be forged or misrepresented, and people linked to the Prime Minister's office were arrested over the leak (FAIR).

Matching later events is what an authentic plan would do. A document written or edited after the fact would match too. Only access to the file and the device can tell the two apart.

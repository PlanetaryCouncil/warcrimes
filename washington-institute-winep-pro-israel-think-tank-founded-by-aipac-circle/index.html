P2The Washington Institute (WINEP): a pro-Israel think tank with an official-sounding name

Founded 1985 · Dossier compiled 27 Oct 2026
  • Its own line: “a balanced and realistic understanding.” Wikipedia's first sentence: “an American pro-Israel think tank.”
  • Founded with AIPAC's support: the founding president was the wife of a former AIPAC president; the first director came from AIPAC's research department.
  • Its director went after the Post's al-Nasr dead-babies report. The Post: “no errors.”

The Washington Institute for Near East Policy is, in the first sentence of its Wikipedia article, “an American pro-Israel think tank”; it was established in 1985 with the support of the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) and the funding of many AIPAC donors, with Barbi Weinberg, wife of former AIPAC president Larry Weinberg, as founding president and AIPAC's former deputy research director Martin Indyk as founding executive director. It describes itself as advancing “a balanced and realistic understanding of American interests in the Middle East,” does not disclose its donors, and in 2024 its executive director Robert Satloff led a public campaign against the Washington Post's report on premature babies found decomposing in al-Nasr Children's Hospital — a report the Post defended as containing “no errors.”

Sounds like a government office. Isn't one.
⚠ Not every Washington Institute paper is propaganda: much of its output is conventional policy research, and its alumni have served presidents of both parties. What we mark as propaganda here is the al-Nasr campaign — an organisation disputing an atrocity report — under this site's definition. No evidence was found that any government directs the Institute; because its donors are undisclosed, outside funding influence can be neither confirmed nor excluded beyond its own statement that it is funded only by US citizens. In its own words vs. the encyclopedia

“to advance a balanced and realistic understanding of American interests in the Middle East and to promote the policies that secure them”
— The Washington Institute, Our Mission & History
“an American pro-Israel think tank … established in 1985 with the support of the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) and the funding of many AIPAC donors”
— Wikipedia, opening lines (also what Google shows in its knowledge panel)

The Institute's history page names its founders but does not mention AIPAC. verified

Who founded it

  • Barbi Weinberg, founding president — former president of the Jewish Federation in Los Angeles; wife of Larry Weinberg, a former AIPAC president. reported
  • Martin Indyk, founding executive director — previously AIPAC's deputy director of research; later twice US ambassador to Israel, assistant secretary of state, and special envoy for Israeli-Palestinian negotiations (2013–14). Died 2024. reported
  • Purpose, per historian Thomas G. Mitchell (2013): research “of a more academic quality” than AIPAC's own publications. No source found shows AIPAC controls the Institute today; that is a critics' claim. reported

Who runs it

A volunteer board appoints the executive director, Robert Satloff (Segal Executive Director). President Moses Libitzky; chairman Jay Bernstein; chairmen emeriti Howard P. Berkowitz, Martin J. Gross, Shelly Kassen, James Schreiber. verified — board page, read 27 Oct 2026.

Board of advisors (Wikipedia, Oct 2024) includes Richard Perle, R. James Woolsey, Condoleezza Rice, Evan Bayh, Martin Peretz, Mortimer Zuckerman and James G. Stavridis; former members include Henry Kissinger, George P. Shultz, Alexander Haig and Jeane Kirkpatrick. reported

The money

Contributions were 82% of 2024 revenue. The donors are not published: US non-profits report major donors to the IRS on Schedule B, which is not public. The Quincy Institute classes WINEP among “dark money” think tanks and notes it advertises “private dinners” for donors of $25,000 or more; it also notes WINEP's policy against foreign-government funding. verified

The revolving door

Staff have moved into senior Middle East posts under presidents of both parties reported:

  • Martin Indyk — NSC, assistant secretary of state, ambassador to Israel, special envoy.
  • Dennis Ross — Clinton's Middle East envoy, Obama adviser; now the Institute's counselor.
  • David Makovsky — State Department negotiating team 2013–14, on leave from the Institute.
  • Dana Stroul — deputy assistant secretary of defense for the Middle East 2021–23; rejoined as research director in 2024.

Supporters read this as expertise; critics read it as a lobby's research arm placing people in government. Both readings fit the facts.

Critics and defenders

  • Mearsheimer & Walt (LRB, 2006; book 2007): WINEP is “part of the core” of the Israel lobby, “funded and run by individuals who are deeply committed to advancing Israel's agenda.” criticism
  • M.J. Rosenberg, former AIPAC staffer: called it an AIPAC “cutout” and criticised broadcasters for presenting its fellows as neutral experts. criticism
  • Rashid Khalidi (Columbia), Al Jazeera 2003: described it as the most important Zionist propaganda tool in the US (translation from Arabic, not checked). criticism
  • Washington Post executive editor Sally Buzbee to Satloff, 2024: his allegations against reporter Hazem Balousha were “inaccurate, irresponsible and deeply unfair”; the Post found “no errors.” verified
  • Defence — Martin Indyk, 1989: “We are friendly to Israel but doing credible research on the Middle East in a realistic and balanced way.” Martin Kramer, 2004: it “accepts funds only from American sources.” reported

H5The “voluntary contribution”: 10,000 shekels a truck, and an 11-day disruption of commercial goods into Gaza

13–25 Oct 2026 · Last updated 30 Oct 2026
  • Both sides agree money was asked for. The fight is over one word: “voluntary.” A request from the ministry that licenses you is not a neutral request.
  • Two different arrests — and an admission. Israel: a merchant's son detained. Gaza's union head, to Masdar News: a trader under house arrest for refusing to sell at 25% cash. Gaza's own communiqué concedes “measures taken against certain traders” — for suspected illicit enrichment, it says.
  • Gaza offered its documents. The communiqué says it is ready to hand over “all relevant documents and testimonies,” and documentation on “the nature and funding of community initiatives,” to international bodies. We found no report of anyone taking it up. We're asking.
  • “Resumed” on 19 October — on paper. Al-Araby Al-Jadeed reports the crossing was then shut or restricted for days more; trucks flowed again on 25 October, at about 130 a day against 160–170 before.
  • The bill went to the hungry. UN OCHA: potatoes up 72%, the food basket up 20% on average.

Israel's COGAT said on 14 October 2026 that Gaza merchants had stopped coordinating commercial imports after Hamas-run authorities demanded about 10,000 shekels (~$3,200) per truck and that about 30% of goods be sold for cash, enforced, it said, by threats and the detention of a merchant's son; Gaza's Government Media Office denied any tax or fee and called the money a “voluntary community initiative” for a national relief fund. The head of Gaza's private truckers union confirmed the Ministry of Economy had asked for the 10,000 shekels — its undersecretary called it a donation to pay civil servants — but said Israel, not the merchants, stopped the trucks; commercial imports were disrupted for about 11 days — sharply reduced rather than stopped outright, by Gaza's own crossings figures — food prices jumped, and we found no published receipt, fee order or audit of the fund.

A “voluntary contribution” requested by the ministry that decides whether you can trade is a strange kind of voluntary. A “merchants' strike” at a crossing only Israel controls is a strange kind of strike. Gazans paid for both.
⚠ Two parties to the war tell this story, and each is weighed as a party: Israel's COGAT controls what enters Gaza and wants no money reaching Hamas; the Hamas-run government needs cash to pay its staff and wants Israel blamed for the shortages. Neither has published a document. The closest thing to independent evidence is Gaza's own truckers union head, and he confirms only that the 10,000 shekels was requested — not that it was compulsory, not the threats, and not Israel's account of who stopped the trucks. The communiqué itself — what the English coverage left out

Al Jazeera, AP and Reuters quoted the Gaza Government Media Office in two or three phrases. The full text — in Arabic on its Telegram channel at 10:02 UTC on 15 October 2026 (part 1, part 2; archived with SHA-256), and in an official English version posted by its Director General on X at 10:21 UTC (English) — says more:

  • It concedes action against merchants. “Any measures taken against certain traders are based exclusively on suspicions and violations warranting legal accountability, including suspected illicit enrichment.” That doesn't confirm Israel's account — but it does confirm that the authorities acted against traders during the dispute, which is what both the detention claim and the house-arrest claim describe. verified
  • It offers proof. Twice: “all relevant documents and testimonies” on the traders' cases, and “all documentation relating to market-regulation measures, the nature and funding of community initiatives.” We found no report of anyone taking it up. open
  • It names whom it wrote to. Egypt, Qatar and Turkey as mediators; Ramiz Alakbarov, acting UN Special Coordinator and Humanitarian Coordinator; Nickolay Mladenov of the Board of Peace; Ali Shaath, head of the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza. We found no published reply. open
  • It doesn't say the 10,000 shekels was never asked of truck importers. It says the sum “relates to” a voluntary fund. Gaza's truckers union head told AP the ministry asked for it per truck, and the ministry's undersecretary called it a donation for civil servants' pay.

Full text of the communiqué (official English, as published)

Published by the office's Director General, Ismail al-Thawabteh, on X: English (15 Oct 2026, 10:21 UTC) and Arabic (same thread); the Arabic also on the office's Telegram 5186/5187. We checked the English against the Arabic: it is faithful. Reproduced unedited; the wording, including “genocidal war” and “the criminal Netanyahu,” is the author's. Archived with SHA-256.

Press Release Issued by the Government Media Office in Response to the Lies and Disinformation Campaign Promoted by the “Israeli Coordinator” to Justify the Ongoing Policies of Siege and Starvation Against the Gaza Strip

We are closely monitoring the blatant disinformation campaign and the false statement issued by the so-called “Israeli Coordinator of Government Activities in the Territories,” through which he seeks, as usual, to promote baseless claims and fabricated accusations concerning the mechanisms governing commercial activity and the entry of goods into the Gaza Strip. He has fabricated narratives alleging that traders are being forcibly charged sums of money, distorted the procedures regulating cash circulation, and attempted to hold the government apparatus in Gaza responsible for the obstruction of truck deliveries.

In response to this clear disinformation campaign by the Israeli occupation, which aims to evade its humanitarian obligations, justify its violations, and threaten to impose further restrictions that deepen the policy of starvation against our people, we have taken the initiative to inform the relevant international and regional parties of the irrefutable facts. Accordingly, we have sent official letters and communications to the following: the mediators—the Arab Republic of Egypt, the State of Qatar, and the Republic of Türkiye; Mr. Ramiz Alakbarov, Acting United Nations Special Coordinator for the Middle East Peace Process and Humanitarian Coordinator; Mr. Nickolay Mladenov, Executive Director of the Board of Peace; and Mr. Ali Shaath, Commissioner-General of the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza.

Accordingly, we categorically reject these fabrications promoted by the Israeli occupation and clarify our position to our great Palestinian people, public opinion, and the international community as follows:

First: We categorically deny imposing any taxes or government fees on commercial trucks. We affirm that we have not collected any taxes or fees on commercial activities over the past three years, in consideration of the exceptional humanitarian and economic circumstances we have endured amid the genocidal war against the population of the Gaza Strip.
As for the amount of 10,000 shekels falsely cited by the occupation's coordinator, it is, in reality, associated with a voluntary community initiative in which a number of businesspeople, public figures, institutions, and companies operating in the Gaza Strip participate through a national fund for community contributions. All of its resources are allocated to supporting the most vulnerable and severely affected groups, including orphans, widows, women in need, children, and students from disadvantaged backgrounds, in addition to financing shelter, sanitation, and emergency relief projects, in accordance with the highest humanitarian standards and considerations.

Second: The measures concerning cash transactions, involving a 30% rate, which the occupation has attempted to misrepresent, were an unavoidable necessity to address severe imbalances in the Palestinian market. These included an acute shortage of cash liquidity and the widespread circulation of worn-out banknotes, which have increased citizens' suffering and disrupted buying and selling activities and the settlement of everyday transactions.
The relevant authority emphasizes that this approach was not a unilateral government decision. Rather, it was adopted in full agreement with, and at the initiative of, the Chamber of Commerce in response to the challenges facing market activity. Reducing these regulatory measures to allegations of “controlling money,” without verifying their nature and objectives, is nothing more than a misleading conclusion devoid of objectivity. The occupation is using such allegations to justify its policies of restricting and starving our Palestinian people. We reaffirm our full readiness to clarify the regulatory basis of these measures to the competent international authorities.

Third: The government apparatus in the Gaza Strip, in fulfillment of its responsibilities, is committed to enforcing Palestinian law and combating the monopolization of essential commodities, price manipulation, and the exploitation of citizens' needs for illicit gain amid the harsh humanitarian conditions. We will not allow our people to become prey to any attempts at monopolization, manipulation, or exploitation.
Any measures taken against certain traders are based exclusively on suspicions and violations warranting legal accountability, including suspected illicit enrichment, and are fully subject to the established legal and judicial procedures applicable in Palestine, particularly in the Gaza Strip. We strongly reject the occupation's attempt to draw sweeping conclusions from individual cases whose circumstances have not been presented in full, and to exploit them to portray these measures as a campaign of coercive extortion. We stand fully ready to provide all relevant documents and testimonies to enable an objective verification of the grounds for these lawful and justified measures.

Fourth: Our highest and most urgent priority is to ensure the regular and sufficient flow of goods. Accordingly, the announced understandings, including those reached through the Sharm El-Sheikh process, oblige the occupation government, headed by the criminal Netanyahu, to fulfill its commitments to allow the entry of aid and supplies, specifically 600 trucks per day.
We firmly reject any reduction in or obstruction of this commitment, or any attempt to link its implementation to baseless allegations concerning the regulation of the domestic market. The government apparatus rejects any attempt to hold it responsible for reducing the number of incoming trucks. The continuation of these restrictions exacerbates the humanitarian and economic crisis and obstructs the provision of food, medicine, and shelter. We call on the mediators and international parties to exert effective pressure to compel the occupation to honor its commitments and refrain from taking any measures that disrupt the flow of commercial goods and humanitarian supplies. Any failure to do so will make the humanitarian situation even more severe and catastrophic than it already is.

Fifth: We call on the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza, the mediators, the United Nations system, and all concerned international parties to examine the occupation's allegations seriously and objectively, and to exercise caution before adopting conclusions or positions based on the Israeli occupation's one-sided and misleading narrative.
We reiterate our openness and readiness to engage directly with international parties and provide all documentation relating to market-regulation measures, the nature and funding of community initiatives, and the legal grounds for holding violators and monopolists accountable, thereby enabling a comprehensive understanding based solely on the facts.

In conclusion, we affirm that regulating markets, combating monopolistic practices, and protecting citizens from exploitation are fundamental national and legal responsibilities, essential to preserving the lifeline of economic activity under the most difficult circumstances. We urge the mediators, the National Committee, and the United Nations to verify the facts through direct communication with the competent authorities in Gaza and to intensify pressure to compel the occupation to open the crossings and allow the entry of 600 trucks per day, without any restrictions or extortion.

— Government Media Office Gaza Strip – Palestine Thursday, 15 October 2026

Same facts, different verbs

Every outlet had the same three facts — a demand, a denial, a halt. Who the sentence makes guilty depends on the verb:

  • Al Jazeera: “Israel halts commercial goods from entering Gaza; Hamas denies tax claims” — Israel acts.
  • AP: “Commercial goods are no longer entering Gaza, raising fears of worsening shortages” — nobody acts; the consequence leads.
  • Times of Israel: “Israel cites Gazan merchants angered by Hamas taxes” — Israel's claim, attributed; the denial's word “voluntary” becomes “taxes.”
  • Alhurra (US-government-funded): “Gaza Traders Halt Shipments, Citing Hamas Extortion Demands” — the traders act; “extortion” in the headline.
  • Jerusalem Post (20 Oct): “Gaza aid shipments resume as Hamas backs down from extortion threats” — these were commercial shipments, not aid; and that Hamas “backed down” rests on merchants' reports COGAT itself says are unverified.
  • Shehab (Hamas-aligned): the government denies fees and warns the claims are being used to justify cutting goods — Israel acts, Hamas is the victim of a smear.

None of these headlines is false. Each is a choice.

Not the first report of payments

The October dispute landed on months of reporting about money taken from Gaza's trade — by Hamas-run bodies, and by others:

  • AP, August 2026 (via Times of Israel): Economy Ministry official Mohammad Barbakh said the Hamas-run government takes up to 20% of the price of smuggled goods if declared, up to 80% if discovered undeclared, and doesn't call it “taxes”; two senior officials in the Hamas administration estimated up to $100 million collected since the ceasefire. The same investigation found Israeli reserve soldiers indicted for smuggling — one commander allegedly paid about $120,000 — and a middleman prosecutors say had “numerous connections within the Israel Police.” Hamas's media office denied the money funds its military wing. reported
  • Alhurra/MBN, 6 October 2026 (link; US-government-funded): anonymous merchants describe a ministry demand of 5 million shekels for trucks brought in late 2025, of which one says he paid 3 million and got no receipt, and a warehouse raid after refusing a 10 million demand. The ministry denies imposing taxes or customs and says it monitors markets for safety and prices; its published notices list seizures but no totals. reported
  • Reuters, February 2026, as summarised by Alhurra: Hamas resumed collecting money from the private sector, including fees on smuggled goods. We could not open the Reuters original. reported

These are earlier, different practices. They make the October allegation plausible; they don't prove it.

The bigger money: who else takes a cut

The 10,000 shekels is the visible fee. It sits inside a much larger trade in access:

  • Israel chooses who may import. Only a short list of Israel-approved traders can bring commercial goods in — the Shms trader says eight. A licence that scarce is worth money to whoever holds it, and leverage to whoever grants it. reported
  • “Coordination fees.” Gaza's Chamber of Commerce says traders paid more than $2.06 billion in “illegal coordination” charges from October 2023 to 26 July 2026 (Gaza Herald, Alhurra). It doesn't publish its method or name who collected the money; the figure is unaudited. reported
  • Mada Masr's “Kings of famine” (original; MEMO summary) describes a profiteering system run by companies close to the Israeli military and the Egyptian state, naming Ibrahim al-Organi's Sons of Sinai group and five Israel-approved Palestinian trading firms. We could not open the original; we rely on the summary. reported
  • Cash is a commodity. Israel restricts the entry of new banknotes; worn notes are refused in shops; electronic payment carries commissions. Whoever can demand cash gains a scarce asset. That is the Hamas government's stated reason for the cash rule — and Israel's stated reason to suspect it. reported

The power vacuum behind it

  • A year after the October 2025 ceasefire, no other authority is running Gaza. The Palestinian technocratic committee (NCAG), formed in January 2026, had not been allowed to enter as of mid-October; the international stabilisation force was not deployed. Hamas's ministries still administer whatever is administered. reported
  • Hamas says it has already let go. On 6 July 2026 it announced it was dissolving the Government Emergency Committee that oversaw Gaza's ministries and handing civil administration to the NCAG — but not its weapons (Al-Monitor). In October the same ministries were still inspecting shops and issuing statements, and the Media Office still speaks of “the government apparatus in Gaza.” reported
  • Hamas needs cash. Its own undersecretary said the money was to pay civil servants. Paying police and clerks is also how an authority stays an authority.
  • Israel needs Hamas to have none. Every shekel that reaches Hamas is, in Israel's framing, a breach of the ceasefire's logic. Its tool is the permit: pay Hamas and you lose the right to import.
  • Merchants are caught between two licences. One authority controls the gate, the other controls the street. Either can end a trader's business.
  • The 600-truck floor hasn't been met by any count we found. The ceasefire floor is 600 trucks a day; COGAT now says Gaza needs about 250; Sheheiber put actual entries near 300, two-thirds commercial. A fee dispute that halves the commercial share hits a supply line that was already short.

What we don't know

  • Whether paying was in practice optional. open No list of payers and refusers, and no account of what happened to refusers, has been published.
  • How many trucks paid, how much was collected, and where it went. open No receipt, signed fee order, or fund accounts.
  • Whether a merchant's son was detained (COGAT) or a trader put under house arrest (Shms), or both — and who they are. open
  • Whether the 30% cash rule (COGAT), the 25% figure (Shms) and the Chamber of Commerce measure (Gaza government) are the same rule. open
  • Who actually stopped the trucks: merchants refusing to coordinate, or Israel conditioning entry on the trader's release. open Both can be true at once.
  • Why the crossing stayed shut for days after Israel announced the resumption on 19 October. open
  • Whether the demand was really dropped, or just paused. open COGAT says the merchants' word is not verification; Gaza's side says there was never a demand to drop.
  • Whether anyone — the UN, the mediators, the NCAG — took up Gaza's offer of “all relevant documents and testimonies.” open
  • What the merchants it acted against are accused of, and whether any charge was brought before a court. open

How to weigh it

  • Something was asked for. Gaza's own truckers union confirms it, and the Hamas-run government does not deny it — it renames it. That much is as solid as this story gets.
  • “Voluntary” is a claim, not a finding. Neither is “extortion.” Both come from parties to the war, and neither has shown a document.
  • The union head's confirmation is narrow. To AP he confirmed the ministry asked for 10,000 shekels per truck; to Masdar, in Arabic, he called Israel's claim “not correct” and passed on the ministry's line that it was a contribution traders are “not obliged” to pay. Both are consistent with a request; neither is proof of compulsion. Quoting him as proof of the whole Israeli story misuses him.
  • “Halt” overstates it. Commercial trucks were cut sharply, not to zero, by Gaza's own counts; what stopped for days was the normal flow, and for a time commercial flour.
  • Keep the fee and the cash rule apart. A 30%-cash sales rule is not a 30% tax.
  • Count channels, not outlets. Most English coverage goes back to one COGAT letter and one interview with Sheheiber, repeated.
  • The people who paid are known. Whatever the truth of the fee, Gaza's shoppers paid it in prices, and its hungry paid it in empty markets.

02Double-tap strike on Nasser Hospital kills journalists and rescuers

25 Aug 2025 · Last updated 12 Nov 2026
  • The second missile was for the rescuers — fired as they pulled bodies from the first.
  • Killed live on air: a Reuters camera streamed its own operator's death.
  • Five journalists from Reuters, AP and Al Jazeera died; the army said it was aiming at “a Hamas camera.”

On 25 August 2025, Israeli forces struck Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis twice in quick succession, with the second strike hitting rescuers and journalists who had gathered at the scene of the first, an attack partly captured on a Reuters live camera feed. Gaza health officials said at least 20 people were killed, including five journalists working with Reuters, the Associated Press, Al Jazeera and other outlets; the Israeli military said the journalists were not the target and that a preliminary inquiry found troops had aimed at what they identified as a Hamas camera, while the UN Secretary-General and press-freedom groups called for an independent investigation.

The double-tap, and what later investigations found

Video from the scene shows how the two waves of the attack unfolded. Reuters said its live camera feed, operated by cameraman Hussam al-Masri from an upper-floor position on the hospital’s eastern stairwell, shut down at the moment of a first strike at about 10:08 local time, which killed him. About nine minutes later, at about 10:17, as journalists and rescue workers gathered on the same stairwell, a further strike hit them; Reporters Without Borders said that second strike was captured live by Al-Ghad TV. BBC Verify, Sky News and NBC News later concluded, from frame-by-frame analysis of the footage, that what first appeared as two strikes was at least four: two in the opening wave, and two near-simultaneous projectiles on the same stairwell in the second.

The World Health Organization’s director-general said at least 20 people were killed, including four health workers and five journalists, and that about 50 others were injured. The journalists, whose deaths were confirmed by the outlets they worked for, were Hussam al-Masri, a Reuters contractor; Mariam Dagga (also Mariam Abu Dagga), a freelancer for the Associated Press and Independent Arabia; Mohammad Salama, a cameraman for Al Jazeera; Moaz Abu Taha, a freelancer who had contributed to several organisations, including occasionally Reuters; and Ahmed Abu Aziz, a freelancer for Middle East Eye. Later AP and Reuters reporting put the death toll at 22. Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu’s office said Israel “deeply regrets the tragic mishap.”

The following day the Israeli military said troops had targeted what they identified as a Hamas surveillance camera used to observe IDF forces, and named six of the dead as “terrorists,” without publishing supporting evidence. A September 2025 Reuters investigation reported that the camera belonged to Reuters — al-Masri had often covered it with a prayer rug — that troops used tank fire, and that they had acted without the required approval of the commander of Southern Command. AP reporting found inconsistencies in the six names, including that two of those named were identified by their employers as a hospital health worker and a Civil Defence driver, and that one name did not appear on the hospital’s casualty list.

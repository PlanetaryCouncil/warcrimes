P3AIPAC: “Voters control elections.” Its super PAC spends the money.

Founded 1954 · Super PAC since 2022 · Compiled 27 Oct 2026
  • Its FAQ: “Does AIPAC control elections? Voters control elections.” Its super PAC spent $14.5 million unseating one congressman.
  • Its About page says “Israel” 18 times in 414 words. “Palestinian” and “Gaza”: zero.
  • October 2026: no more endorsements or direct PAC cheques as its support turns toxic. The super PAC, with unlimited money, keeps going.

The American Israel Public Affairs Committee is, in Wikipedia's first sentence, “an American pro-Israel lobbying group”; it was founded in 1954 by Isaiah L. Kenen, a lobbyist for the Israeli government, as the American Zionist Committee for Public Affairs, and took its current name in 1959. After decades of leaving campaign money to its members, it launched its own PAC in late 2021 and the United Democracy Project super PAC in January 2022, which spent about $14.5 million to defeat Rep. Jamaal Bowman in 2024 — more than any outside group had spent on a House race — and $8.5 million against Rep. Cori Bush, both critics of Israel's war on Gaza; on 21 October 2026, with its backing now a liability in Democratic primaries, AIPAC said it would stop endorsing candidates and making direct PAC contributions, while the super PAC carries on.

The “PAC” in the name was never a PAC. Since 2021 it has run two.
⚠ AIPAC says it is “funded exclusively by Americans” and is “not funded nor directed by, nor lobby for, the Israeli government”; no evidence was found here that contradicts that, and it is not registered as a foreign agent. Spending totals differ by tracker and cut-off date (OpenSecrets, Sludge, FEC); we give each figure with its source. The backlash, and the retreat

  • 2025: Democrats including Deborah Ross and Seth Moulton said they would no longer accept AIPAC donations (The Nation, via Wikipedia). reported
  • 21 Oct 2026: in a letter to members AIPAC said it would stop endorsements, direct PAC contributions and earmarking, warning “the challenges we are confronting will continue to grow,” and promised to “prioritize increasing direct, organized contributions from individual Americans to candidates.” United Democracy Project continues — and was already running ads against El-Sayed, now the Democratic nominee (NPR). verified

Critics and defenders

  • Mearsheimer & Walt (LRB, 2006): AIPAC is “a de facto agent for a foreign government” with a “stranglehold” on Congress. criticism
  • Rosen–Weissman case: in 2005 AIPAC fired its policy director Steve Rosen and analyst Keith Weissman amid an FBI investigation; Pentagon analyst Lawrence Franklin pleaded guilty to passing them classified information; charges against the two were dropped in 2009. reported
  • Defence — AIPAC: bipartisan, “funded exclusively by Americans,” and “there is no such thing as unconditional aid to Israel.” self-description

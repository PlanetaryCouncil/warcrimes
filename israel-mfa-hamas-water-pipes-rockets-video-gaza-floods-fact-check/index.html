P5“Outrage should be directed at Hamas”: a 2020 rocket video, reposted for Gaza's 2026 floods

Israel MFA post: 9 Nov 2026 · footage aired by Al Jazeera: 13 Sep 2020 · pipes described by Sinwar: 4 Nov 2019 · Last updated 10 Nov 2026
  • The video is real — and Hamas's own. The Al-Qassam “Military Media” watermark runs through it; its scenes match Al Jazeera's “What Is Hidden Is Greater”, aired 13 September 2020.
  • The pipes aren't Gaza's sewers. Sinwar said in 2019, and Qassam told Al Jazeera in 2020, that they came out of the ground in the former settlements, left behind in 2005.
  • The floods have nearer causes. UN: thousands of families flooded, pumps without fuel, 150,000 tents waiting outside Gaza. Israel: 91,000 tents delivered, Hamas to blame.

On 9 November 2026, as the first winter storm flooded Gaza's tent camps, Israel's Foreign Ministry posted a 64-second Al-Qassam Brigades video of men digging up pipes and turning them into rockets, writing that “when the rains come and sewage floods Gaza's streets, outrage should be directed at Hamas.” The footage is genuine Hamas propaganda, but it isn't new: it matches an Al Jazeera documentary aired on 13 September 2020 and already circulated in October 2023 as “EU-funded water pipes”, and the pipes are the ones Yahya Sinwar said in 2019 were dug out of the former Israeli settlements, not Gaza's sewers or storm drains. The UN and aid groups attribute this week's flooding to a water, sewage and drainage network damaged in the war, pumps without fuel or spare parts, and tents held outside Gaza; Israel says 91,000 tents have gone in and blames Hamas.

True ingredients, false recipe. Hamas did turn pipes into rockets, and bragged about it on camera; under a ban on importing metal, it chose rockets. But the pipes in this video came out of the sand of the former settlements years before this war, and the sewage in the tents this week comes from a network damaged in the war and pumps without fuel. Blaming 2020 footage for 2026 floods is the propaganda move. Pretending Hamas never spent scarce metal on rockets is the opposite one.

Whose pipes?

  • Sinwar, 2019: “the pipes that the occupier left behind when it left the Gaza Strip” — irrigation lines from Israeli farms in Gush Katif (via MEMRI).
  • Qassam, via Al Jazeera, 2020: old water pipelines “previously used to steal Gaza's groundwater and pump it to illegal settlements” (QNN's summary).
  • JNS, 2020: “metal water pipes left behind by Israel when it unilaterally withdrew from Gaza in 2005” (JNS).
  • Israel MFA, 2026: “water pipes … infrastructure meant to serve Gazans.”

Hamas, Al Jazeera and a pro-Israel outlet agree on where the pipes came from: settlement-era lines, buried in the sand after 2005. The Foreign Ministry's phrase is arguable, not invented — abandoned water lines could have served Gaza's farms — but nothing here is the sewage or storm-drain network now overflowing.

What is flooding Gaza this week

  • The storm: from 7 November. At least five people, three of them children, killed when a sand embankment collapsed on tents west of Deir al-Balah; about 26,000 households asked for help; more than 6,000 family shelters destroyed; water 1.5 metres deep in places (UN News, citing OCHA). reported
  • The UN and aid groups: “much of Gaza's water, sewage and drainage network was damaged during the war”; pumps are failing without fuel, engine oil and spare parts; aid groups hold about 2,800 tents in Gaza against a need of 121,000, while more than 150,000 wait outside; they need Israeli permission to bring in pumps, vacuum trucks and machinery (IRC, UN). reported
  • Israel: security officials say COGAT, the Board of Peace and the UN brought in 91,000 new tents, tarpaulins and thousands of truckloads of clothes and blankets; that Hamas exploits aid to rearm; and that materials for permanent rebuilding and mobile homes stay out until Hamas disarms (Jerusalem Post, 8 Nov). reported
  • The Board of Peace itself called the winter aid “insufficient” and pressed Israel to let more in (Jewish News). reported

Same footage, different verbs

  • Qassam / Al Jazeera, 2020: the resistance recycled the occupation's leftovers under siege.
  • Sinwar, 2019: the brothers removed from the ground what the occupier left behind.
  • JNS, 2020: the terrorist group salvaged pipes left by Israel.
  • Israel MFA, 2026: Hamas is digging up water pipes; infrastructure is sacrificed; outrage should be directed at Hamas.
  • A repost, 2026: Hamas dug up the drainage system, released it on October 7th; Israel is not to blame for any flooding.

Each step drops a fact: first where the pipes came from, then when, then what they were. By the last version, 2020 footage of settlement pipes has become proof about a 2026 storm.

How to weigh it

  • The fair point. Hamas built rockets from whatever metal it could find and boasted about it; the choice of rockets over anything else is its own, and Sinwar said so on television.
  • The unfair one. The video doesn't show Gaza's sewers or drains, and nothing ties it to this winter. Using it to answer “why are the tents flooding?” swaps the question.
  • Both sides have a case on aid. Israel's 91,000 tents and the UN's 150,000 tents held outside can both be true. The dispute is about need and pace, and only a joint, published count would settle it.
  • Why it's on this desk. The Foreign Ministry is a party to the war; its post uses real footage to make a claim the footage doesn't support. That is what extra caution is for.

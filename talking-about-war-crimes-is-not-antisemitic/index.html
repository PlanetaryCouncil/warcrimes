Talking about war crimes is not antisemitic

Read 27 Oct 2026 · every claim below links to its source
  • Antisemitism is hostility to Jews as Jews. A war-crimes record is about what a state's army did.
  • Some of the loudest people documenting it are Jewish and Israeli: Israel's own rights groups, a Holocaust historian.
  • The playbook for blurring the two is predictable: make something that sounds official, and hope nobody checks.

Antisemitism is real, and it is dangerous. That is exactly why it matters that the word means something. The Jerusalem Declaration on Antisemitism, launched on 25 March 2021 by 210 scholars of antisemitism, Holocaust studies, Jewish studies and related fields, defines it as “discrimination, prejudice, hostility or violence against Jews as Jews (or Jewish institutions as Jewish).” Every page on this site is about the conduct of a state, its army and its officials — named, dated and sourced.

Criticising a government is not hating a people. Confusing the two is the point of the exercise.

Who says so

  • Scholars of antisemitism. The Jerusalem Declaration lists, as examples that “on the face of it, are not antisemitic”: “evidence-based criticism of Israel as a state” — adding that “it is not antisemitic, in and of itself, to compare Israel with other historical cases” — and criticism “that some may see as excessive or contentious.” It now has more than 400 signatories. jerusalemdeclaration.org
  • Israel's own human-rights groups. On 27 July 2025 B'Tselem published Our Genocide, and Physicians for Human Rights–Israel published a parallel report on the destruction of Gaza's health system — the first time Israel-based rights groups called their state's conduct genocide (+972 Magazine). The IDF called the claims “unfounded.”
  • A Holocaust historian. Omer Bartov, Israeli-born professor of Holocaust and genocide studies at Brown University, in The New York Times, 15 July 2025: “I'm a Genocide Scholar. I Know It When I See It.”
  • The World Court. In South Africa v. Israel the International Court of Justice ordered provisional measures under the Genocide Convention on 26 January 2024. A court hearing a case is not antisemitism.

The playbook

It is very predictable. Every step below is documented on this site, with sources.

  1. Pick a name that sounds official. “The Washington Institute” sounds like a government office; it is a pro-Israel think tank founded with AIPAC's support. “HonestReporting” sounds like a press watchdog; its mission is fairness in journalism “as it impacts Israel.” “Public Affairs Committee” sounds like civics; AIPAC runs a super PAC. “The Hanover Institute for Public Policy” sounds like a university centre; it is paid for by Israel's government advertising agency, through a registered foreign agent.
  2. Call yourself balanced. The Washington Institute: “a balanced and realistic understanding.” Wikipedia's first sentence: “an American pro-Israel think tank.” Exhibit A.
  3. Keep the money quiet. The Washington Institute does not publish its donors (Quincy Institute: “dark money”). In 2026 AIPAC's super PAC funded pop-up PACs with neutral names — “Elect Chicago Women” — whose donors surfaced only after the votes.
  4. Attack the messenger, not the record. HonestReporting insinuated that Gaza photographers knew of 7 October in advance, then admitted it had no evidence. The Washington Institute's director demanded the Washington Post retract its report on babies left to decompose at al-Nasr; the Post found “no errors.” Neither disputed that the babies died.
  5. Answer the questions before anyone asks them. In nine days of August 2026 the Hanover Institute published 124 reports — 560,000 words — headlined as the questions people type into chatbots: “Which Countries Recognize Israel's Actions in Gaza as Genocide?” The funder's contract is on file at the US Justice Department.
  6. Spend to remove the critics. About $14.5 million to unseat Rep. Jamaal Bowman, $8.5 million against Rep. Cori Bush — both critics of the war on Gaza. Its ads often don't mention Israel.
  7. Fold “Israel” and “Jews” into one word. When criticism of a government is filed under hatred of a people, nobody has to answer the criticism. An “antisemitism” institute funded by Israel's government files its reports on Gaza's siege and East Jerusalem under “Israel & Anti-Zionism.” The Jerusalem Declaration exists to pull the two apart.

What we do instead

Name the institution, quote its own words, link the original, date the source, label its stance — and show the other side's case next to ours, under “Official government sources — exercise extra caution” on every incident page. Don't trust, verify. Check your jurisdiction.
